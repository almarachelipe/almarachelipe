ADVANCED PRINT DESIGN

A home textile print collection rooted in the wooden cutlery craft of Udayagiri, Nellore. The intricate carved motifs of this centuries old craft were translated into a surface language for the table, with the final collection sitting across a range of grounds and palettes.

The Craft

Udayagiri wooden cutlery is believed to have originated in the 14th century in the Nellore district of Andhra Pradesh. Each piece carries intricately hand carved motifs, originally made for royal patronage and now surviving as a reflection of the community's artistry and patience. The cutlery became the primary visual source for the project.

Sketches

Initial sketches translated the carved cutlery forms into line, focusing on the ornament pattern across each handle and the silhouette of each piece.

Rendering

Rendering tests looked at how the same motif could be read differently in filled, outlined and tonal versions, each one carrying a different weight on the surface.

Motif Development

The motif was then broken down into its parts and worked into a vocabulary of repeats, borders and ground textures. These iterations tested scale, density and the balance between figure and ground before any colour was applied.

First Iteration

An early wallpaper application tested the full motif as an all over repeat, settling the direction and spacing of the collection.

Collection

The resolved collection carries the cutlery motif across a set of coordinated pieces for the table. The teal ground anchors the collection, with brown, red, cream and burgundy sitting around it as alternative palettes.

Colour Ways

The collection was then composed into alternative palette directions, from the resolved teal into deeper browns and warmer yellows. Each composition sets the motif against its ground alongside the swatches that build it.
