From carved cutlery to printed cloth

How the wooden cutlery of Udayagiri was studied, drawn, taken apart into motifs and built back up into a collection ready for screen printing.

The Craft: Udayagiri Wooden Cutlery

Believed to have originated in the 14th century, the wooden cutlery craft of Udayagiri, a small town in the Nellore district of Andhra Pradesh, is known for its intricately hand carved spoons, forks, knives and bowls. It flourished under royal patronage, particularly under the Nawabs of Arcot in the 19th century, and what began as a utilitarian practice became an art form that turned everyday objects into pieces of refined elegance.


Each piece blends Persian influenced motifs with local craftsmanship. The artisans carve soft woods from nearby forests using only simple hand tools, and over centuries have mastered the balance between function, durability and ornament. Today both men and women continue to preserve and renew the craft.

Sketches

The cutlery was first drawn by hand, piece by piece, to understand the silhouette of each spoon and fork and the ornament carved along each handle.

Rendering Styles

The same pieces were rendered in several ways, from solid silhouette to wood grain to stipple, to see how each treatment changes the weight of the motif on a surface.

Motif Library

The carving was then taken apart. Every ornament on the handles was redrawn as a separate line motif, giving a library to build repeats, borders and grounds from.

Wallpaper Iterations

The wallpaper was the first product explored. Iterations moved from tight arrangements of whole cutlery, through positive and negative blocks, to a looser falling layout with a patterned ground.

Texture and Colour

Textures made in different mediums were laid under the motif, first in grey and then in the browns of the wood itself, to test how much surface the ground could carry before it competed with the cutlery.

Repeats from the Motif Library

The line motifs were put into repeat as small all over patterns, to serve as grounds and coordinates for the rest of the collection.

Product Iterations

Each product was planned in line first: where the border sits, how heavy the centre is, and how the pieces relate to one another on a laid table.
