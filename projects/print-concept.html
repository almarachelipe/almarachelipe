Tendrils

SURFACE DESIGN  ·  PRINT CONCEPT

A continuation of two courses, Surface Design and Print Concept, that moved from early botanical study and jaal exploration into a fully developed home textile print. The final rendering was built in response to Jayasri Burman's painting Weeping Grace, carrying its density and jewel toned palette into a repeating surface.

Plant Study

The project began with a study of Poranopsis paniculata, a woody climber from the morning glory family. Its tendrils, small white flowers and heart shaped leaves became the botanical reference for the jaal.

Jaal Development

The plant was translated into a vocabulary of bel, buta and jaal. Early iterations tested how the vine could carry rhythm, how the buta could sit inside and outside the flow, and how the tendrils could knit into a continuous ground.

Layouts

The resolved jaal was placed into product layouts, testing how density, border and buta placement could sit together across the surface of a textile.

Rendering

Pencil and texture rendering tested how weight, density and shadow could be built into the jaal before any colour was introduced.

Art Inspiration

For the print concept portion the inspiration shifted to Jayasri Burman's painting Weeping Grace. Her work carries intricate detailing, a jewel toned palette and layered forms, qualities that fit the challenge of building a complex, textile ready jaal.

Trials

The jaal moved through several colour trials, each one adjusting proportion, saturation and the balance of motif to ground. The final trial brought all the elements of the painting into a settled repeat.

Brand Study

A short study of The Shop looked at how a brand translates traditional textile aesthetics into contemporary surface design, drawing on their palette and motif language to frame the direction of the final collection.

Final Collection

The resolved print sits across a blue ground, with the jaal, buta and bel coordinated into a set of layouts for the table.
