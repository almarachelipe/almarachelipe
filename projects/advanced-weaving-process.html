Process

Blue Damsel

From idea to weave

How earlier fabric samples became painted compositions, how one composition was blocked and simulated, and how it was set up and woven on the loom.

01

Where It Began

The project picks up from my Fabric Construction course, where the Banded Demoiselle, a damselfly with dark blue bands on its wings, had been my inspiration. I chose a few of those fabric samples and used them as the starting point for new surfaces.

02

Free Compositions

On the first day I painted compositions with no rules at all, moving between organic and geometric forms to explore layout, rhythm and how textures sit together.

03

Compositions Under a Constraint

On the second day one rule was added: only geometric shapes, nothing organic. It pushed the compositions towards a tighter, more structured language.

04

Digitisation

The selected compositions were taken into digital form so they could be scaled, recoloured and planned for weaving.

05

Selecting and Scaling the Artwork

Each fabric construction sample was paired with the hand painted composition it led to, and the artwork was set at 12 x 18 inches for final development.

06

Digital Compositions

Three compositions were redrawn digitally at that size.

07

Blocking

Each artwork was broken into grid units and its columns grouped into blocks, A, B and C, to bring the design down to something a loom can weave.

08

Weavepoint Simulation

The blocked design was built in Weavepoint to plan the interlacement, see how it would read as cloth, and arrive at the draft and lift plan.

09

Yarn and Calculations

Warp and weft requirements, sett and repeats were calculated, and the yarns were chosen against the artwork.

10

Warping

11

Setting Up the Loom

The warp was set up on the loom with the help of Masterji: drafting, denting, and then a buffer woven in before the sample began.

12

Weaving

The piece was woven with extra weft laid in by hand where the artwork called for it.
