Process

Ikat

From graph to tied and dyed warp

How the two graphs were tied and dyed into the warp, and how that warp was woven into one long stole.

01

From Artwork to Graph

Ikat cannot hold a curve, so each motif was redrawn square by square on a grid. Every square stands for a group of warp threads and a measured length to be tied, and the stepped edges are what give ikat its feathered outline once woven.

02

Warping

The 2/60s cotton warp was wound on the warping frame and divided into sections, one for each column of the graph.

03

Sectioning and Marking

The sections were grouped and stretched flat on a frame, and the graph was marked onto the threads row by row.

04

Tying

Every area that had to resist the dye was wrapped tightly by hand, following the graph.

05

Dyeing

The warp was dyed with vat dyes, one colour at a time. After each bath, the parts that had to keep their colour were tied again and the rest opened for the next.

06

Opening the Ties

When the last colour had dried, the ties were cut away and the pattern showed on the threads for the first time.

07

Setting the Pattern

The sections were laid out in order and shifted thread by thread until the motif lined up across the warp.

08

On the Loom

The patterned warp was stretched to its full length, beamed, and drawn through the healds and reed.

09

Weaving

The same warp was woven in a series of structures and weft colours, so the motif changes character from one section of the stole to the next.

10

Off the Loom

The finished stole, cut from the loom.
